# Garden.io Website Positioning Research Notes

## Scope

- Region focus: North America (United States and Canada).
- Audience focus: multi-bed home growers and homesteaders with regenerative intent.
- Date anchor: March 9, 2026.
- Goal: convert qualified users to waitlist without exposing proprietary implementation detail.

## Positioning Decisions Used on the Website

1. Position Garden.io as a category-defining workflow, not a feature bundle.
- Narrative: living notebook + planning intelligence.
- Avoid: generic "task app" framing.

2. Lead with best-fit audience and anti-audience.
- Best fit: growers managing meaningful complexity (multiple beds, seasonal planning, mixed systems).
- Not fit: ornamental-only and enterprise ERP buyers.

3. Use outcome-first messaging above the fold.
- Emphasize calm, confidence, continuity, and trust.
- Delay deep mechanics (ranking models, data weighting, inference internals) until qualified conversations.

4. Keep conversion friction low.
- Keep CTA focused on email capture for early access.
- Support trust with clear communication on email usage.

## "Show Value, Not Internals" Public Messaging Rule

Public site should publish:
- who the product is for and not for
- problem and outcome framing
- workflow surfaces (My Property, Calendar, My Plants, Plant Catalogue, Ambient AI)
- reasons to join now (early access cohorts and product shaping)

Public site should not publish:
- proprietary recommendation ranking logic
- full model architecture and tuning details
- complete scoring feature lists and weighted variables
- full internal dataset enrichment pipeline

This balances clarity for buyers with defensibility for the product.

## Information Architecture Chosen

1. Hero + immediate signup (clarity and fit first).
2. Why now / why this problem matters.
3. Audience fit and anti-fit qualification.
4. Product surfaces and workflow symmetry (space, time, knowledge).
5. Public/private disclosure boundary statement.
6. FAQ and second waitlist CTA.

## Competitor Pattern Observations

Current garden-planning competitors often emphasize either:
- visual planning utilities without long-horizon memory, or
- broad feature lists with weak audience qualification.

Garden.io differentiation on site messaging:
- strongest fit statement for multi-bed regenerative workflows
- calm guidance language instead of productivity pressure
- memory + planning intelligence framed as the paid wedge

## Copy Guardrails

Do say:
- living notebook
- plan by bed and zone
- weather-aware weekly focus
- memory that compounds over seasons

Avoid saying:
- generic "AI chatbot"
- enterprise farm ERP claims
- one-size-fits-all messaging for all gardeners
- feature-heavy copy without clear user outcomes
